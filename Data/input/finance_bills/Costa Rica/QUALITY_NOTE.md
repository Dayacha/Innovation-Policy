# Costa Rica Quality Note

## Bottom Line
- Costa Rica is not a stable long-run panel if "R&D budget" is interpreted loosely.
- The original budget files change the object being measured across years.
- A dense extraction is therefore not the same thing as a comparable series.

## Main Comparability Problem
- The originals mix several different types of budget evidence:
  - annual international quotas (`CATIE`)
  - institutional transfer bundles (`CONICIT`, `PCII`)
  - programme totals (`MICITT`, some `INTA`, modern `INCIENSA`)
  - narrow sub-lines or earmarked university transfers (`UCR`)
- These should not all be treated as the same kind of annual R&D observation.

## Conservative Audit Rule
- Keep only observations that are traceable and reasonably comparable.
- Prefer:
  - explicit programme totals
  - institution-level transfer bundles
  - direct institutional lines with a clear R&D object
- Exclude:
  - salary rows
  - generic ministry objects
  - `ING-*` revenue tables
  - broad university operating transfers
  - narrow university sub-lines used as proxies for an institution
  - third-party transfers merely labeled under another institution's law

## Conservative Comparable Backbone
- `CATIE`: 2011, 2013, 2017, 2020, 2021, 2024, 2025
- `CONICIT`: 2010, 2021
- `INTA`: 2011, 2013, 2021
- `MICITT`: 2017, 2021, 2024, 2025
- `INCIENSA`: 2024, 2025
- `PCII`: 2024, 2025

## Explicitly Not Comparable Enough
- `UCR` research-linked sub-lines
- `ITCR / TEC`
- `FEES`
- partial `CONICIT` years sourced only from `ING-*` pages or narrow transfer fragments
- `INTA` years represented only by third-party transfers under the INTA law
- old `INCIENSA` transfer-only years when later years are programme totals

## Interpretation Guidance
- If Costa Rica looks sparse after cleaning, that is usually a methodological feature, not necessarily a parser failure.
- The current series is intentionally conservative and traceability-first.
